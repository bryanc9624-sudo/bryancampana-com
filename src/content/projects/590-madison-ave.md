I authored the signage system for the amenity floor at 590 Madison Avenue — sign types, information hierarchy, ADA compliance, and the wayfinding logic that connects them.

I worked from what the architects had shared: photographs of the materials, floorplans, renderings, and a few pages of their discovery deck. The palette of stone, glass, and metal came out of those references, and I designed the system to sit inside it as though it had been specified alongside the interior. The contrast between the substantial materials and the diffuse ones became the system's own hierarchy, separating what stays fixed from what directs you through the floor. All of it had to satisfy regulatory requirements, tenant branding, and wayfinding at once.
